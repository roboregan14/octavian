Octavian: privacy policy

Effective date: 9 November 2026 Who we are: Octavian is made by Robert O'Regan, an individual developer ("I", "me"). In this policy "the app" means Octavian on iPhone and its companion app on Apple Watch. Contact for privacy questions: roboregan14@outlook.com

The short version

  • Octavian has no account and no sign-in.
  • Your workouts, runs, weights and settings are stored on your phone (and on your Apple Watch for the runs and workouts it records).
  • The app sends nothing to me or to any server. I have no server. It has no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third-party code that collects anything.
  • Your data can still leave your phone by the routes listed below, each one chosen by you or switched on by you: your Apple Watch, Apple Health, iCloud Drive copies, a training partner's phone, files you export or share, and reports you choose to send me.
  • It uses the camera only if you take a photo of a workout plan, and Apple Health only if you say yes.
  • It does not use your microphone, contacts or calendar, and it cannot read your photo library.

I receive nothing from the app. I see something only if you send it to me.

What the app keeps on your iPhone

Your workouts and sets (exercises, reps, weights, times), your runs, walks, rows, swims and rides, your programmes and plans, the weigh-ins and body measurements you type in, your settings (including your first name if you enter one), your rank and statues, and your chosen colour scheme, figure and sounds.

This is stored in the app's own storage on the phone. It is covered by your phone's normal protections (passcode, encryption when locked) and by your own iPhone backups to iCloud or your computer, if you have those switched on in iOS Settings. Those backups are between you and Apple.

Apple Health

Octavian asks permission before it touches Apple Health. You can change your answers at any time in the Health app (Sharing → Apps → Octavian) or in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Health.

What it reads (if you allow it): - your workouts and their routes, from Octavian and from other apps (to show your history and progress); - heart rate, resting heart rate and date of birth (to work out your heart-rate zones); - active energy, distance (walking and running, swimming, cycling, rowing, paddling, skiing), swimming stroke counts, cycling power and cadence (for the runs, swims and rides you do); - body weight, waist circumference and body fat percentage (to show your progress); - steps and stand hours, only if you switch on "Daily movement".

What it writes (if you allow it): - the workouts, runs, walks, rows, swims, rides and ski-erg sessions you record, with their routes, distance, heart rate and active energy, so they count in Fitness and your activity rings; - the bike power and cadence your Watch records on an indoor ride; - the weight, waist and body fat you type in the app.

What happens to it: Health figures are used to show you your own information. The app does not send them to me or to any server, never uses them for advertising or marketing, never sells them, and leaves them out of the iCloud copies (see below). They travel only by the routes in this policy: between your phone and your Watch, inside the files you export or share (the full backup and the spreadsheet export hold your heart rate and calories), and in your own iPhone backups. The app never writes made-up or estimated figures into Health.

Apple Watch

The Watch app records workouts and runs with Apple Health. It passes your sets and runs, with their heart rate, calories and route, to your iPhone, and your iPhone sends the workout and your settings to it, over Apple's own phone-to-watch connection. Nothing goes anywhere else.

Location

Used only while you are using the app, and only for outdoor runs and walks you record: to measure distance and draw the route. The route is stored on your phone and, if you allow it, saved to Apple Health. Indoor sessions use no location. The app declares a background-location mode so a run keeps recording when you lock the screen; it is used for nothing else. The post-run map is drawn with Apple Maps; Apple receives the map area being displayed, under Apple's own privacy policy.

Motion

The app uses the phone's step counter (Apple's pedometer) to measure an indoor run or walk carried on the phone. The step counts stay on the phone. It also uses the phone's motion sensors to notice that the phone is propped or lying down (to switch to its larger "floor" screen). That reading is used on the spot and not stored.

Camera and microphone

Camera: used only when you choose TAKE A PHOTO to put a photo of a workout plan into the app. The app reads the words in the photo, on your phone, with Apple's text recognition. It does not keep the photo and does not send it anywhere. If you choose CHOOSE A PHOTO instead, iOS's picker gives the app only the one photo you pick.

Microphone: not used. The app asks for no microphone access. It only plays sounds and music.

Photos

The app can save the poster of a workout you choose to share into your Photos library. It has add-only access and cannot read or list your photo library. The app never takes or keeps photographs of you.

Notifications

The app asks to send notifications (for example, "Rest over"). They are scheduled on your phone; there is no server sending them. Some are marked Time Sensitive so they can break through a Focus you have allowed it to. While a workout is under way, the Lock Screen shows the workout and rest to anyone who can see your phone. You can switch notifications off in iOS Settings → Notifications.

Local network and training with a partner

To train together on two phones, the app finds your partner's phone on the same Wi-Fi, or over Apple's peer-to-peer Wi-Fi (Apple calls this "local network" access, and iOS asks you first). The first time, one phone shows a six-digit code and the other types it in. After that the two phones talk directly to each other over an encrypted link and remember each other (the keys live in your phone's Keychain, on that phone only). Over the link, the other phone is told: your first name, your chosen figure and colour scheme, the workout and exercises being done, the current set, weight and rest time, and your finish figures (minutes, kilograms lifted). No Health data and no location is sent. Nothing goes over the internet. You can "Forget" a partner at any time.

While you are offering a workout to a partner, the offer itself is not encrypted. Any device nearby on the same Wi-Fi or peer-to-peer Wi-Fi can see your first name, the workout's name, your figure and the turn rule, until the offer ends. The six-digit code is used once to pair; it is not a full password system, so pair with people you trust, not in a crowded place.

iCloud Drive copies

If you are signed in to iCloud and iCloud Drive is on, the app writes dated backup copies of your workout history and plans into your own iCloud Drive, in the folder Octavian, so that a lost phone does not take your history with it. If you have added a training partner who shares your phone, their name and sets are in the copies too. Run maps, sounds and the app's other choices are not. These copies are stored by Apple in your account. I cannot see them.

Health and body figures are removed first. Copies leave out: calories, heart rate, heart-zone times, bike power and cadence, swim stroke counts, your typed weigh-ins and body measurements, and your weight goal. If the app cannot remove them, it writes no copy at all and says so in Settings → Your data. What stays in the copies is your workouts' dates and times, exercises, sets, reps and weights, run distances and times, any notes you typed, gym names and plans. Restoring from a copy brings back your workouts but not the removed figures (your weights and body figures saved in Apple Health come back from Apple Health).

You can see, move or delete the copies in the Files app. With iCloud Drive off, nothing goes to iCloud; the app still keeps its own recent copies on your phone.

Files you make and share yourself

  • Back up everything and the CSV export make a file that you save wherever you choose. These files are complete, including health figures such as heart rate and calories. Treat them as you would any personal file.
  • When you share a workout, a plan, a route or a poster, you choose who gets it. The recipient sees what is in it (a plan's exercises, a route's places, a poster's picture and figures), and a workout you share can include health figures. I never see it unless you send it to me.
  • Importing a workout file from another app, or a plan pasted from an AI chat tool, is read on your phone. The app's "copy the prompt" button puts text on your clipboard (it can include your recent exercises, weights and gyms, so you can ask a chatbot of your choice for a plan); you decide whether to paste it anywhere. The app reads the clipboard only when you press its Paste button.

Music and sounds

The app plays its own sounds, and your own music or podcasts keep playing alongside (it lowers them briefly for a sound). You can paste a playlist link; pressing the ♪ button hands that link to Spotify or Apple Music (or your browser) to open. The app does not read your music library and receives nothing back. It notices whether a phone call is in progress, only to avoid playing over it; it does not see who is calling.

Crash and performance reports

The app has no crash-reporting service and sends no reports by itself. iOS can give the app a diagnostics report of its own crashes (Apple's MetricKit); the app keeps it on your phone. The app also keeps a log of its Watch button presses. From Settings → About you can share these reports with me if you want to help fix a fault. If you do, I receive them, and they describe how the app crashed or behaved on your phone; they do not hold your workouts. If you use TestFlight, Apple may separately pass me crash reports and feedback you chose to send through TestFlight, under Apple's rules.

Who else sees anything

Nobody, through me. The app contains no third-party analytics, advertising, social or crash-reporting code, and it makes no connections to any server of mine. The only parties involved are Apple (Health, iCloud Drive, iCloud Backup, Apple Maps, TestFlight/App Store), under Apple's own policies, your training partner's phone, and people you choose to send something to, including me if you send me a report.

Keeping and deleting your data

Everything lives on your devices or in your own accounts, so you control it: - Delete a workout or a plan: inside the app. - Delete everything: delete the app (this removes its data from the phone and Watch). Then delete the Octavian folder in iCloud Drive (Files app) and any files you exported. - Apple Health data: managed in the Health app. Deleting Octavian does not delete what it saved there; you can remove it in Health. - Withdraw permission: at any time in iOS Settings → Octavian, or Privacy & Security. - Requests to me: I hold none of your data unless you sent me a report, so there is usually nothing for me to delete or send you; if you write to me I will say so and help if I can.

Children

Octavian is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect anything from anyone.

Changes

If the app ever starts collecting anything, I will update this page and the app's App Privacy details before it does. The date at the top shows the latest version.
