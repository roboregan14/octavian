Octavian: privacy policy

Effective date: 9 November 2026 Who we are: Octavian is made by Robert O'Regan, an individual developer ("I", "me"). In this policy "the app" means Octavian on iPhone and its companion app on Apple Watch. Contact for privacy questions: roboregan14@outlook.com

The short version

  • Octavian has no account and no sign-in.
  • Your workouts, runs, weights and settings are stored on your phone (and on your Apple Watch for the runs and workouts it records).
  • The app does not send your workout or Health records to a server operated by me. I have no server. It has no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third-party code that collects anything.
  • Apple's own services carry some things, under Apple's privacy policy: Apple Health, the Watch connection, Apple Maps, your iPhone backups, and TestFlight (see "Crash and performance reports").
  • Your data can also leave your phone by routes you choose: files you export or share, and reports you choose to send me.
  • It uses the camera only if you take a photo of a workout plan, and Apple Health only if you say yes.
  • It does not use your microphone, contacts or calendar, and it cannot read your photo library.

The app itself sends me nothing. I see a report or a file only if you send it to me, with one exception while the app is in testing: Apple's TestFlight passes me crash reports and testing information automatically (see below).

What the app keeps on your iPhone

Your workouts and sets (exercises, reps, weights, times), your runs, walks, rows, swims and rides, your programmes and plans, the weigh-ins and body measurements you type in, your settings (including your first name if you enter one), your rank and coins, and your chosen colour scheme, figure and sounds.

This is stored in the app's own storage on the phone. It is covered by your phone's normal protections (passcode, encryption when locked) and by your own iPhone backups to iCloud or your computer, if you have those switched on in iOS Settings. Those backups are between you and Apple.

Apple Health

Octavian asks permission before it touches Apple Health. You can change your answers at any time in the Health app (Sharing → Apps → Octavian) or in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Health.

What it reads (if you allow it): - your workouts and their routes, from Octavian and from other apps (to show your history and progress); - heart rate, resting heart rate and date of birth (to work out your heart-rate zones); - active energy, distance (walking and running, swimming, cycling, rowing, paddling, skiing), swimming stroke counts, cycling power and cadence (for the runs, swims and rides you do); - body weight, waist circumference and body fat percentage (to show your progress); - steps and stand hours, only if you switch on "Daily movement".

What it writes (if you allow it): - the workouts, runs, walks, rows, swims, rides and ski-erg sessions you record, with their routes, distance, heart rate and active energy, so they count in Fitness and your activity rings; - the bike power and cadence your Watch records on an indoor ride; - the weight, waist and body fat you type in the app.

Where the figures it writes come from: from sensors (your Watch's heart rate, energy and distance, your phone's GPS), from your phone's step counter (an indoor run or walk carried on the phone has its distance worked out from your steps, so that distance is an estimate), or from you (a swim or a weigh-in you type). Corrections to recorded cardio sessions change Octavian's record without rewriting the original Health workout; you can delete that workout in the Health app. Reopening and finishing a strength workout that this iPhone previously saved can replace its own Health entry with the finished one.

What happens to it: Health figures are used to show you your own information. The app does not send them to a server operated by me, never uses them for advertising or marketing, and never sells them. They travel only by the routes in this policy: between your phone and your Watch, inside the files you export or share (the full backup and the spreadsheet export hold your heart rate and calories), and in your own iPhone backups. The app itself does not put them in iCloud Drive (this version makes no iCloud Drive copies; see below), but a file you export can be saved there, or anywhere else, if you choose.

Apple Watch

The Watch app records workouts and runs with Apple Health. It passes your sets and runs, with their heart rate, calories and route, to your iPhone, and your iPhone sends the workout and your settings to it, over Apple's own phone-to-watch connection. Nothing goes anywhere else.

Location

Used only while you are using the app, and only for outdoor runs and walks you record: to measure distance and draw the route. The route is stored on your phone and, if you allow it, saved to Apple Health. Indoor sessions use no location. The app declares a background-location mode so a run keeps recording when you lock the screen; it is used for nothing else. The post-run map is drawn with Apple Maps; Apple receives the map area being displayed, under Apple's own privacy policy.

Motion

The app uses the phone's step counter (Apple's pedometer) to measure an indoor run or walk carried on the phone. The step counts stay on the phone. It also uses the phone's motion sensors to notice that the phone is propped or lying down (to switch to its larger "floor" screen). That reading is used on the spot and not stored.

Camera and microphone

Camera: used only when you choose TAKE A PHOTO to put a photo of a workout plan into the app. The app reads the words in the photo, on your phone, with Apple's text recognition. It does not keep the photo and does not send it anywhere. If you choose CHOOSE A PHOTO instead, iOS's picker gives the app only the one photo you pick.

Microphone: not used. The app asks for no microphone access. It only plays sounds and music.

Photos

The app can save the poster of a workout you choose to share into your Photos library. It has add-only access and cannot read or list your photo library. The app never takes or keeps photographs of you.

Notifications

The app asks to send notifications (for example, "Rest over"). They are scheduled on your phone; there is no server sending them. Some are marked Time Sensitive so they can break through a Focus you have allowed it to. While a workout is under way, the Lock Screen shows the workout and rest to anyone who can see your phone. You can switch notifications off in iOS Settings → Notifications.

Local network and training with a partner

Training with a partner happens on one iPhone between you, with one Apple Watch (the phone owner's). The screen shows whose set it is; your partner's first name, figure and sets are kept on this phone, apart from yours, so that you can send them their workout afterwards if you choose (see "Files you make and share yourself"). The app does not use the local network or Apple's peer-to-peer Wi-Fi: it does not look for other devices nearby, it sends nothing to another phone, and iOS never asks you for local network access.

iCloud Drive copies

This version of the app makes no copies in iCloud Drive. Octavian does not automatically create iCloud Drive copies in this version. It keeps its recent copies on your phone only.

Ordinary iPhone backups and files you explicitly export are separate. Your iPhone backups (to iCloud or your computer, if you have them on) include the app's storage on the phone; they are between you and Apple. Exported files can contain Health figures such as heart rate and calories (see "Files you make and share yourself").

Copies made by earlier test builds: some earlier test builds wrote dated copies into a folder with the app's name (Octavian, or Princeps for a few November builds) in your own iCloud Drive. This version does not delete them automatically. You can see, move or delete them in the Files app (iCloud Drive → Octavian or Princeps). They are stored by Apple in your account; I cannot see them.

Files you make and share yourself

  • Back up everything and the CSV export make a file that you save wherever you choose. These files are complete, including health figures such as heart rate and calories. Treat them as you would any personal file.
  • When you share a workout, a plan, a route or a poster, you choose who gets it. The recipient sees what is in it (a plan's exercises, a route's places, a poster's picture and figures), and a workout you share can include health figures. I never see it unless you send it to me.
  • Importing a workout file from another app, or a plan pasted from an AI chat tool, is read on your phone. The app's "copy the prompt" button puts text on your clipboard (it can include your recent exercises, weights and gyms, so you can ask a chatbot of your choice for a plan); you decide whether to paste it anywhere. The app reads the clipboard only when you press its Paste button.

Music and sounds

The app plays its own sounds. Short cues may lower other audio; the finale song can pause it while the song plays. You can paste a playlist link; pressing the ♪ button hands that link to Spotify or Apple Music (or your browser) to open. The app does not read your music library and receives nothing back. It notices whether a phone call is in progress, only to avoid playing over it; it does not see who is calling.

Crash and performance reports

The app has no crash-reporting service of its own. iOS can give the app a diagnostics report of its own crashes (Apple's MetricKit); the app keeps it on your phone. The app also keeps a log of its Watch button presses. From Settings → About you can share these reports with me if you want to help fix a fault. If you do, I receive them, and they describe how the app crashed or behaved on your phone; they do not hold your workouts.

TestFlight: while you use TestFlight, Apple automatically shares crash reports and testing information with me, including installation and session counts and device details (such as model and iOS version), under Apple's TestFlight rules. They do not hold your workouts. With public-link invitations, Apple does not display your name or email to me. Email invitations and feedback can identify you: if you were invited by email, or you send feedback or screenshots through TestFlight, I can see who you are. I use this information to test and improve Octavian.

What the app's own reports hold (the ones you share from Settings → About): the app's own log lines about what it was doing, how it crashed, and your device's model and iOS version. The report itself holds no name or account, unless you type one in a note you add. A report you email me arrives from your email address, so the email can identify you.

How I keep what I receive: I keep crash reports, TestFlight's testing information and the reports you send me (TestFlight's from Apple, and those you share from the app) to find and fix faults, for as long as I maintain the app. If you want yours deleted, write to roboregan14@outlook.com and I will delete them.

Who else sees anything

I pass nothing on to anyone else. The app contains no third-party analytics, advertising, social or crash-reporting code, and it makes no connections to any server of mine. The only parties involved are Apple (Health, the Watch connection, iCloud Backup, Apple Maps, TestFlight/App Store), under Apple's own policies, and people you choose to send something to, including me if you send me a report.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your workouts, plans and Health figures live on your devices or in your own accounts, so you control them (what I keep is in "Crash and performance reports"). Each place is cleared on its own: - A workout or a plan: delete it inside the app. - The phone: deleting the app removes its data and its recent copies from the phone. Copies already in your iPhone backups stay in those backups until they are replaced or you delete them (iOS Settings or your computer). - The Watch: deleting the iPhone app normally removes the Watch app too; if it stays, delete it on the Watch. A workout the Watch saved to Apple Health stays in Health. - Apple Health: deleting Octavian does not delete what it saved there; remove it in the Health app (Sharing → Apps → Octavian, or each workout). - Files you exported or shared: wherever you saved or sent them; delete them there. The app cannot reach them. - A training partner: their name and sets are kept on this phone only; remove a partner in Settings → With a partner → Partners on this phone, or delete the app. If an earlier test build paired this phone with another phone, that pairing key stays in this phone's Keychain unused (iOS can keep an app's keys in the Keychain after the app is deleted). - Withdraw permission: at any time in iOS Settings → Octavian, or Privacy & Security. - Requests to me: I hold none of your data except crash reports, TestFlight's testing information and reports you sent me (see "Crash and performance reports"); if you write to me I will say what I hold, if anything, and delete it.

Children

Octavian is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes

If the app ever starts collecting anything beyond what this page describes, I will update this page and the app's App Privacy details before it does. The date at the top shows the latest version.
